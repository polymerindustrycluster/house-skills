# Room artifacts — house forms

The artifacts this skill produces, with the house-tested form of each. Every one is a
ROOM artifact: it cites the evidence lane and never substitutes for it.

## Talk outline (the working file)

One file per talk, top to bottom:

```
ROOM: <named audience, size, date, occasion>
ACTION: doers → <verb>; suppliers → <verb>; influencers → <verb>; innovators → <verb>
BIG IDEA: <one arguable sentence with the stake in it>

BEATS (cards, in order):
  [IS]    <claim> | evidence: <page/source> | say-verbatim: "<sound bite>"
  [COULD] <claim> | evidence: <page/source> | say-verbatim: "<sound bite>"
  ...
  [STAR]  <type: dramatization/sound bite/visual/story/statistic> | placement rationale
  [ASK]   <per-role asks for the roles PRESENT, each a verb + a date>
  [SUM]   <the false ending: the three numbers and the asks, restated in four
           sentences. This is the beat you DROP when the slot collapses — [BLISS]
           survives every cut.>
  [BLISS] <the world after adoption — the reward picture; NOTHING follows this
           except the Big Idea restated>  (Duarte's order: asks, then reward — R p.42)

Q&A protocol: questions open after [SUM]; after the last question, RE-CLOSE with
[BLISS] and the Big Idea line — the room leaves on your sentence, not on the last
questioner's. (The two-endings rule, HBR p.93: overruns cost the summary, never the
close.)

THREE NUMBERS: <n1 — room-scaled form> / <n2 — …> / <n3 — …>
SOURCES: <evidence pages / claim files each number traces to>
```

The `IS`/`COULD` marks are not decoration — they are the sparkline audit. Three of the
same mark in a row = restructure before writing a single slide.

## Beat library / menu (a versioned setlist source, not a talk)

When a talk is drawn from a maintained library of beats (the Spotlight pattern: ~66
beats, per-beat cards, a run-of-show clock that selects among them), the library is
an INVENTORY and the talk is its SETLIST. Rules the first live audit paid for:

- **One canonical number line per beat entry.** Cards, clocks, and summaries
  REFERENCE it and never restate it — every restated copy is a future drift (a
  degrees-per-year average was corrected in the beat entry and survived, stale, in
  its card).
- **Corrections propagate by sweep, not by memory**: when a beat's number or framing
  is corrected anywhere, grep the WHOLE artifact for the old form — the correction
  that misses the run-of-show clock misses the one section the presenter rehearses
  from.
- **The gauntlet runs on the frozen setlist**, never on the menu: the three-number
  cap, the sparkline audit, and the cold listener are all unrunnable against an
  inventory. Freeze first, audit second.
- The library carries its own hygiene line per beat: source tier (gated / prototype),
  last-verified date, and any DO-NOT-SAY bans in the beat entry itself.
- **STAR candidates are inventory too**: a library may name a STAR candidacy per
  beat, but the setlist spends at most one (two only under the declared-registers
  rule) — a kit names candidacy; it never awards the slot. Same logic as the
  three-number cap: retention rules bind the run-of-show, never the shelf.

## Teach-back card (house form; the guard block was DERIVED from a library failure)

The unit of transfer: one card per beat, written so a NON-PRESENTER can deliver the beat
cold. If the card needs its author in the room, it failed.

```
BEAT: <title, ≤8 words>
SAY: <2–4 sentences, spoken register, interpretation before magnitude,
      every number in room-scaled form>
IF ASKED: <the one hard question this beat invites, and its honest answer,
           including "we don't know" where true>
NUMBER GUARD:
  EXACT: <the derived figures, with source page — the line you may never drift from>
  ROOM-SCALED: <the approved approximation for speech ("about one in ten", "roughly
               $71,000 a year") — deliberately approximate, computed ONCE, here>
```

Provenance, honestly: ~38 of these were built for the Spotlight before this skill
existed, and they carried SAY and IF-ASKED but **no NUMBER GUARD block** — the
EXACT/ROOM-SCALED split below was derived from that gap after a stale number survived
in a card its beat entry had already corrected. The block is the fix, not the
victory lap. Rules learned building them:
- The SAY block is the sound bite plus its runway — never a bullet list. Bullets are
  for the author; sentences are for the mouth.
- IF ASKED is mandatory. A beat without its hard question written down gets improvised
  at the podium, and improvised answers are where numbers bend. Incident: "everything
  international" was an improvised intensifier; the card now carries the true split so
  the speaker can't reach for the false one.
- NUMBER GUARD's two lines mark the boundary between scaling for the room (required —
  the ROOM-SCALED line, deliberately approximate per Duarte's scale doctrine) and
  drifting from the derivation (banned — the EXACT line). The card does the
  approximation ONCE, correctly, so the podium never improvises it, and the
  traceability audit (review-gates Layer 2) treats a ROOM-SCALED line as guarded, not
  drifted.

## One-page ask (funder / board)

Duarte's one-page Recommendation Tree (*DataStory* Appendix), house-adapted. Top band =
the three-act executive summary in three columns: **Situation** (their frame, their
numbers) — but/and — **Complication** (the measurement that must change) — so —
**Resolution** (the DataPOV, with the quantified outcome and the specific ask:
amount/decision/date). Lower band = a WHAT / WHY / HOW grid, one column per sub-action:
WHAT takes the verbs, WHY starts with "Because…" and names the data that changes, HOW
carries the process steps. Sources footer. The reader must reach the ask without
turning anything over; detail rides behind a link to the evidence lane, never inline —
an ask that tries to also be the evidence does both jobs badly (the slideument
failure, in print).

Duarte's required contents that a polite draft always omits (*DataStory* Ch. V): the
**notable objections stated and rebutted in the artifact** (readers who don't see
their objection assume you missed it), the **abandoned alternative and why** you
rejected it, and every prediction's assumptions in the form **"This is true if…"**.
When an objection or alternative is genuinely confidential (a negotiation position,
a personnel matter), the one-pager carries the concise disclosure ("two alternatives
were evaluated and rejected on cost; detail available") and the controlled backup
carries the substance — never zero, never a leak.

## Executive summary (for the leave-behind, replacing the slideument)

The three-act DataStory as one prose unit — Situation, but/and, Complication, so,
Resolution — written in **full sentences, no bullets** (*DataStory* Ch. IV/VIII), first
page of the Slidedoc. Conclusion-first is house doctrine (Duarte orders the summary
first without stating the rationale): a reader, unlike a room, can leave whenever
they want. The Slidedoc is not only a leave-behind — it is also the standalone
executive handoff ("send me five slides"), built to argue the case with nobody in
the room to speak for it. Then: the three numbers with one line of support each →
risks/costs priced honestly → objections and abandoned alternatives → sources. One
page; the evidence room is the appendix.

## Briefing (FYI, no action) — the DOWNSHIFT form

Gate 1's third outcome. When there is honestly no action (a status update, a courtesy
briefing), say so and downshift: no manufactured what-could-be, no STAR moment, no
ask, and gate 2 relaxes from *arguable* to *accurate* — the Big Idea becomes a summary
sentence that is simply the truest one-line account of the situation. Keep:
room-scaled numbers, sound-bite discipline, traceability. Gauntlet: Layers 2 and 3
only (a briefing can still bend a number or lose a cold reader; it just has no
sparkline to audit). A briefing dressed as a story erodes the trust that real asks
spend — and a real ask dressed as a briefing to dodge the gates is worse.

## What this skill does NOT produce

Evidence pages, articles, methodology boxes, charts (route to `pic-charts`), or any
artifact whose reader is anonymous and hostile rather than assembled and addressable.
The moment an artifact will be published to strangers, it changes lanes.
