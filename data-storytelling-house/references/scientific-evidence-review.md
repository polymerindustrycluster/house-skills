# Scientific evidence review for presentations

Use when the room must judge whether a scientific finding supports a conclusion,
application, or next step. This is a communication check on supplied, verified
evidence, not a substitute for scientific peer review or a fresh analysis. An issue
with the underlying evidence is referred to its owner under the boundary law.

## Choose the room shape honestly

For a technical review, use question → evidence and material methods → limits →
judgment or next test. An accurate question and bounded conclusion satisfy the
Big-Idea gate; "not yet established" can be the right conclusion. Do not require
an arguable thesis, persuasion alternation, STAR moment, or optimistic ending.
If the actual task is a funding decision, use the decision/ask form while retaining
the scientific support and limits that determine that decision.

## Preserve what changes the conclusion

For each load-bearing scientific claim, keep a short record in the existing working
file or refer to an existing claim record. Fill only applicable fields:

| Field | What the presenter and reviewer need |
|-|-|
| Claim and source | Exact proposition; source identifier, version/date and locator |
| Observation | What was measured, on which unit, under which conditions |
| Comparison | Control, baseline or benchmark; whether groups/conditions are comparable |
| Scale and measurement | Units, denominator, independent units versus repeat measurements; laboratory/pilot/field scope |
| Uncertainty | Reported range/interval and its meaning; or explicitly not reported |
| Inference | What the observation supports, what is hypothesized, and credible alternative explanations supplied by the evidence |
| Decision boundary | What remains untested and what would change the proposed next step |

This table is a **house safeguard**, not a checklist attributed to Schwabish.
An absent field is unknown, not zero or permission to invent a value. Refer missing
analysis to the evidence owner; do not compute a convenient interval or infer
significance while building the presentation.

Apply one relevance test to methods: **could omitting this change the audience's
confidence, interpretation, or decision?** If yes, explain it beside the finding.
If no, it may belong in backup or be cut. A method's novelty can itself be the
scientific result. Laboratory conditions, controls, failed replications, or a
limitation that changes the recommendation cannot be dismissed as effort-display.

## Keep observation, explanation, and promise distinct

- Match the verb to the source's evidence: observed, associated, estimated, modeled,
  hypothesized, or demonstrated under the specified conditions. Story order does
  not turn association into causation.
- Keep a mechanism illustration distinct from experimental confirmation of that
  mechanism. A schematic arrow needs a supported relationship or an explicit
  hypothesis label.
- Preserve the scope of the evidence. A favorable laboratory comparison alone
  establishes neither field durability nor manufacturing economics, customer
  qualification, safety, or regional economic impact.
- Retain the comparator and denominator behind a favorable percentage. Do not turn
  an unreported uncertainty estimate into certainty or a count of repeat readings
  into a count of independent samples.
- Include contradictory evidence or supplied alternative explanations that change
  the conclusion. Unresolved objections stay unresolved; a rebuttal cannot be
  manufactured to satisfy an ask template.

For a fictional example, suppose a source reports different batch averages, while
the batches also used different test conditions and supplied no uncertainty estimate.
The slide can report the bounded comparison. It cannot claim the formulation caused
the difference, infer statistical significance, or promise the same improvement at
production scale. A follow-up comparison may be proposed; its result remains unknown.

## Decide where detail belongs

The main explanation carries the result and any qualifier needed to interpret it.
Put supporting derivations, secondary methods and additional questions in reachable
backup. A source link alone is not a substitute for an essential qualifier. Material
qualifications that must survive a screenshot, recording, or forwarding belong in
the visible artifact, not only the speaker notes.

Retain the existing source numbers and figure bundle. Improve legibility through
selection, emphasis, and sequencing; do not silently redraw, recompute, or remove
uncertainty to make a chart fit. If a template's display limits cannot hold the
scientific content honestly, split the explanation or choose a readable format.
Explain the tradeoff in the working file rather than silently dropping evidence.

Close with what the evidence establishes, what remains uncertain, and the decision
or next question appropriate to the room. A scientific review may end with an open
question or a reason not to proceed. No manufactured benefit or deadline is needed.

## Review the audience's judgment

Use review-gates Layer 2 for traceability and Layer 4 for the strongest counter-case.
Add a Layer 3 question requiring the listener to distinguish a supported conclusion
from an attractive unsupported one, or to identify what a changed test condition
would invalidate. Set answer criteria before the listener sees the question.

Review both the central claim and the listener's scope/causality/uncertainty inference
under [review-gates Layer 3](review-gates.md#layer-3--cold-listener-the-only-layer-that-cant-be-faked).
A materially wrong judgment needs revision even when the key number was recalled.

## Sources and scope

Passages checked in local copies on 2026-09-08:

- Jonathan Schwabish, *Better Presentations*, local edition copyright 2017,
  pp. 11–14 (PDF 26–29): the middle of a talk can carry data, methods and technical
  detail; pp. 15–17 (PDF 30–32): depth depends on the audience; p. 20 (PDF 35): retain
  conclusion-relevant caveats in the close. File: `Schwabish-2016-Better-Presentations.pdf`.
- Amy Elsbree and Amy Peake, chapter 2 in *Elevate the Debate*, edited by Jonathan
  Schwabish (2020), pp. 29–30 (PDF 45–46): different audiences need different detail
  and formats, and recommendations must fit operating constraints. File:
  `Schwabish-2021-Elevate-the-Debate.pdf`; the filename year is not the book year.
- Existing [Duarte frameworks](duarte-frameworks.md), §11: objections, alternatives,
  and prediction assumptions. In a scientific review these may remain open rather
  than become an argument for an already-chosen recommendation.

These passages support purposeful methods and audience-specific detail. The claim
record, scientific inference safeguards, and judgment test are house adaptations.
They do not authorize stronger scientific claims than the consuming source supports.
The held-book location and PDF-page convention are in [scientific explanation](scientific-explanation.md#sources-and-scope).
